# Essay Types

> **At a glance.** Every essay type below is a named composition of the shared
> [components](./components.md) — the same building blocks recombine across types rather
> than each type inventing its own vocabulary. Not sure which type fits? Start at
> [Which Essay Type Do I Need?](./routing.md).

- **[Descriptive Essay](./descriptive.md)** — Makes the reader experience a person, place, or thing rather than just being told about it.
- **[Expository Essay](./expository.md)** — Explains or informs on a topic using facts and examples while staying neutral — the writer's opinion isn't the point.
  - [Cause and Effect Essay](./cause-effect.md) — Explains why something happened, or what resulted from it, by demonstrating the causal mechanism rather than asserting a link.
  - [Compare and Contrast Essay](./compare-contrast.md) — Explains two subjects by showing how they resemble and differ from each other on the same points.
  - [Definition Essay](./definition.md) — Establishes what a term means beyond its dictionary line, especially for terms that are contested, abstract, or narrower/wider than common usage assumes.
  - [Process / How-To Essay](./process.md) — Lets the reader actually perform a procedure by walking through it in the order it must happen.
- **[Literary Analysis Essay](./literary-analysis.md)** — Argues for an interpretation of a text, grounded in the text's own words rather than a summary of what happens in it.
- **[Narrative Essay](./narrative.md)** — Tells a true story from the writer's experience that arrives at a point, not just an account of what happened.
- **[Persuasive / Argumentative Essay](./persuasive.md)** — Takes a position on a debatable issue and moves the reader toward agreeing with it or acting on it.
