# Descriptive Essay

> Makes the reader experience a person, place, or thing rather than just being told about it.

## Blocks

### [Dominant Impression](./blocks/dominant-impression.md)

> "The workshop smelled like a place where things got fixed, not made."

### [Sensory Detail](./blocks/sensory-detail.md)

> "Sawdust caught the light from the one high window and never seemed to fully settle."

### [Figurative Language](./blocks/figurative-language.md)

> "The vise sat at the bench's edge like a hand permanently closed."

### [Conclusion](../../blocks/closing/conclusion.md)

**For this type:** Ties back to the dominant impression rather than summarizing facts about the subject.

> "Faster checkout is a real gain — but a library that measures only speed will miss what it's trading away."

## Organizational patterns

Named ways to arrange this type's blocks, chosen by the writer:

### Spatial

Moves through the subject by physical layout (near to far, top to bottom, room to room).

### Chronological

Describes a subject as it changes or is experienced over time (a storm passing, a meal being eaten).

### Order of Importance

Orders details from least to most (or most to least) central to the dominant impression.

## Pitfalls

- Details listed without a unifying impression — reads as a list, not a picture.
- No organizing principle, so the reader can't reconstruct the whole from the parts.
- Figurative language that describes the writer's cleverness more than the subject.
