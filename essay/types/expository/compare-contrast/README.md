# Compare and Contrast Essay

> Explains two subjects by showing how they resemble and differ from each other on the same points.

A subtype of [Expository Essay](../README.md).

## Blocks

### [Thesis Statement](../../../blocks/opening/thesis-statement.md)

**For this type:** Names both subjects and the basis of comparison, not just 'X and Y are similar and different.'

> "The library's new self-checkout system cuts wait times but removes a point of human contact many patrons relied on."

### [Comparison Points](./blocks/comparison-points.md)

> "On cost: self-checkout runs $0.02/transaction in maintenance; a staffed register runs $0.85 in wages."

### [Conclusion](../../../blocks/closing/conclusion.md)

**For this type:** States why the comparison matters, not just a recap of what was compared.

> "Faster checkout is a real gain — but a library that measures only speed will miss what it's trading away."

## Organizational patterns

Named ways to arrange this type's blocks, chosen by the writer:

### Block (Subject-by-Subject)

Covers all points for subject A, then all the same points for subject B.

### Point-by-Point

Alternates A and B within each point of comparison before moving to the next point.

## Pitfalls

- Comparing on inconsistent points across the two subjects (apples to oranges within the essay itself).
- Ending without saying why the comparison is worth having made.
- Block structure so long the reader has forgotten subject A's details by the time subject B arrives — point-by-point usually reads better past a couple of comparison points.
