# Persuasive / Argumentative Essay

> Takes a position on a debatable issue and moves the reader toward agreeing with it or acting on it.

## Blocks

### [Hook](../../blocks/opening/hook.md)

> "The library's self-checkout machines process a book in four seconds. The librarian they replaced also asked how your week was going."

### [Thesis Statement](../../blocks/opening/thesis-statement.md)

**For this starter:** Must be an arguable claim, not a stated fact — this is what distinguishes it from the expository thesis.

> "The library's new self-checkout system cuts wait times but removes a point of human contact many patrons relied on."

### [Evidence](../../blocks/body/evidence.md)

**For this starter:** Reasons for the claim, each backed by supporting evidence.

> "Average checkout time dropped from 90 seconds to 12 seconds after the machines were installed (library annual report, 2025)."

### [Counterargument](./blocks/counterargument.md)

> "Opponents point out that self-checkout eliminates jobs for the library's part-time staff."

### [Rebuttal](./blocks/rebuttal.md)

> "But the same budget that funded the machines also funded two new community-programming positions — the jobs moved, they didn't disappear."

### [Call to Action](./blocks/call-to-action.md)

**For this starter:** A concrete next step for the reader, not just a restated opinion.

> "Ask your branch what it did with the labor savings before you decide whether the tradeoff was worth it."

### [Conclusion](../../blocks/closing/conclusion.md)

> "Faster checkout is a real gain — but a library that measures only speed will miss what it's trading away."

## Pitfalls

- A strawman counterargument — refuting a weak version of the opposing view instead of its strongest form.
- Evidence given with no explicit link back to the claim it's supposed to support.
- A thesis that's actually a statement of fact, leaving nothing to argue.
