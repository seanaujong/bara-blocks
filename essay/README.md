# Essay Building Blocks

> School teaches a small, stable set of essay starters, and each one decomposes
> into a small, stable set of blocks — a thesis, a hook, a counterargument, a resolution.
> The point of writing it down this way isn't to publish a taxonomy — it's so that, faced with a
> piece of writing, you can name what you're actually trying to do and see which blocks are
> likely to help.

## Essay starters

- **[Narrative](./starters/narrative/README.md)** — tells a true story from experience, building to a point.
- **[Descriptive](./starters/descriptive/README.md)** — makes the reader experience a subject through sensory detail.
- **[Expository](./starters/expository/README.md)** — explains or informs, staying neutral.
  - **[Compare and Contrast](./starters/expository/compare-contrast/README.md)** — how two things are alike and different.
  - **[Cause and Effect](./starters/expository/cause-effect/README.md)** — why something happened, or what it caused.
  - **[Process / How-To](./starters/expository/process/README.md)** — walks through doing something, step by step.
  - **[Definition](./starters/expository/definition/README.md)** — establishes what a term actually means.
- **[Persuasive / Argumentative](./starters/persuasive/README.md)** — argues for a position, or moves the reader to act.
- **[Literary Analysis](./starters/literary-analysis/README.md)** — argues for an interpretation of a text.

## Blocks

**[blocks/](./blocks/)** is the shared glossary, one file per block — a thesis
statement, a hook, a counterargument, and so on each show up across more than one essay starter
above, described once here rather than re-explained on every page.
