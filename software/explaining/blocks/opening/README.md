# Opening

> **At a glance.** [Summary](./summary.md), [Problem Statement](./problem-statement.md), and
> [Decision and Rationale](./decision-and-rationale.md) are described separately because each
> answers a different question — what happened, what was wrong, why this fix — but a document
> doesn't have to give each its own sentence or section. They often fuse into a single opening
> paragraph, the same way a five-paragraph essay's intro fuses a hook and a thesis into one
> paragraph instead of two.

## Worked example

Each sentence on its own:

> **Summary:** We're replacing staffed checkout registers with self-checkout kiosks across all
> branches.
>
> **Problem Statement:** Checking out a book today requires a staffed register, which
> bottlenecks at peak hours and doesn't scale to new branches without adding staff.
>
> **Decision and Rationale:** Self-checkout kiosks cost the most up front of the options we
> considered, but they're the only one that removes the bottleneck entirely and scales to new
> branches without adding staff.

Fused into one opening paragraph, in the same order:

> We're replacing staffed checkout registers with self-checkout kiosks across all branches.
> Today, checking out a book requires a staffed register, which bottlenecks at peak hours and
> doesn't scale to new branches without adding staff. Self-checkout kiosks cost the most up
> front of the options we considered, but they're the only one that removes the bottleneck
> entirely and scales to new branches without adding staff.

The order doesn't change — summary, then problem, then decision — only the formatting does.
